    About this tool

    How do I find a missing DOI for a BibTeX entry?
    Paste your .bib and click Find missing DOIs and fill fields. It searches Crossref by your title and first author, checks each match against your title, author, and year, and only fills in a DOI when it is confident. It leaves one blank rather than guess wrong. Some works, such as many books, theses, and older papers, have no DOI at all.
    How do I remove duplicate entries from a .bib file?
    Click Clean with De-duplicate on. It merges entries that share a DOI, citation key, or title, and it flags likely duplicates that use different keys so you can check those yourself.
    My URL does not show up in the bibliography. Which URL button do I use?
    It depends on your style. biblatex and modern styles read a plain url field, so use URLs to url field. Classic BibTeX styles (plain, abbrv, unsrt, alpha) ignore the url field, so the link only appears if it is in howpublished or note as \url{...}. Use URLs to \url{} in note for those. Both buttons keep the link in one place. The \url form needs \usepackage{url} or hyperref in your preamble.
    Is my .bib file uploaded anywhere?
    No. All parsing and cleaning happen in your browser. DOI lookups go straight from your browser to Crossref, so your library is never sent to our servers.
    What does “Protect Title Capitals” do?
    BibTeX lowercases title words unless they are wrapped in braces. This wraps acronyms and all-caps words (e.g. DNA, NASA, RNA-seq) in {} so they keep their capitalization.
    Where does the DOI data come from?
    Crossref for most works, and DataCite for Zenodo-style DOIs.
    Does it work with both BibTeX and biblatex?
    Yes. It cleans standard .bib files used by both, and it can convert URL fields to suit either a classic BibTeX style or biblatex.
